You don't need a reinvention. You need release notes.

Careers change in versions, not overnight. A practical take on rethinking your work: what changed, what you added, and how to tell the story of the next version of you.

The idea

Think like software, not like a movie montage.

Apps don't reinvent themselves overnight. They ship small updates, the occasional big change, and notes that explain what's different. Careers work the same way, so this site borrows the format.

01

Small releases beat big leaps

A new skill, a side project or a stretch assignment counts. Most rebrands are a run of small updates, not one dramatic jump.

02

Big changes are normal

A new field, a layoff or a promotion can break the old story. That's a new version, not a failure.

03

The story needs updates too

Your bio, résumé and "so what do you do?" answer drift out of date. Patch them every time you ship.

Try it

Write your career changelog

List the versions of your career so far. Seeing it laid out makes patterns obvious, and it's a great starting point for a bio or an interview answer. Everything stays in your browser.

Tip: a big change usually bumps the first number (v2.0 to v3.0). Small updates bump the second.

    Framework

    Your career story in one sentence

    When someone asks what you do, the answer should sound like your latest version, not a list of every job you've had.

    I help [who] with [what problem] by [how you do it], and lately I've been [what's new].
    1. Start with who you help. A team, a type of customer or a kind of person. It works even if your title doesn't say it.
    2. Name the problem, not the task. "Reports" is a task. "Leaders not knowing where the money goes" is a problem.
    3. Add how in a few words. This is where your skills show up.
    4. End with your newest release. It signals where you're heading and gives people something to ask about.

    Before: "I'm an accountant at a mid-size firm."
    After: "I help small business owners understand where their money goes by turning messy books into simple monthly reports, and lately I've been building dashboards so they can check it themselves."

    Not a reinvention. Just the next release.
